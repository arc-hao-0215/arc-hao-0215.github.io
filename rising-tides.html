Rising Tides, Resilient Lives

Water-Resilient Community Design

Kampung Melayu, JakartaFlood Resilience · Civic Infrastructure · Collective Life2025

SOM China Fellowship · Winner

Project proposition

How can flood-prone infrastructure become a civic sanctuary?

In Kampung Melayu, a river is both a source of urban life and an intermittent interruption to it. Rising Tides, Resilient Lives imagines a shared architectural ground in the space between the Ciliwung River, existing settlement and elevated transport infrastructure.

Rather than treating protection and everyday use as separate questions, the proposal gathers routes, workshops, markets, dwellings and ecological systems into one adaptive civic network. Its sectional organisation changes with water levels: a public landscape in ordinary conditions, and a connected framework for access and refuge during flooding.

01 / Urban context

A river within a sinking metropolis.

The Ciliwung passes through densely inhabited communities where flooding, changes to drainage and exposure of existing settlements intersect. The design reads the river edge and elevated railway as shared infrastructure rather than leftover space.

The condition

Flooding changes more than the waterline.

When water occupies the ground, access to neighbours, services and shared places can become as important as the condition of individual buildings. Any spatial response needs to address these connections, not just lift structures above a presumed level.

Metropolitan subsidence and coastal processes are part of Jakarta's wider water story, but the riverine flood exposure at Kampung Melayu must be distinguished from the city's northern tidal conditions.

Two seasons, two ways of living with water.

The rhythm of the river shapes when the ground can be occupied, how neighbours move, and where collective life takes place.

01 / Musim Kemarau

Dry Season

When the water recedes, streets and river edges become everyday public spaces for gathering, trade and movement. Access to shared services and the river landscape remains essential.

02 / Musim Hujan

Wet Season

As water rises, activities and routes shift to higher ground. Continued access, places of refuge and connections between neighbours become more important as low-lying areas flood.

02 / Design strategy

From isolated flood protection to collective infrastructure.

The proposal reframes the river edge as a continuous public system. New ecological space, shared rooms, accessible routes and adaptable housing are conceived together, so that everyday uses and emergency needs can be understood as related spatial conditions.

01 / RESTORE

Make room for water

Wetlands and river-edge landscapes create space to examine filtration, ecological continuity and flood storage alongside urban occupation.

02 / ADAPT

Keep essential access above the ground

Raised routes and adaptable dwellings propose continuity of movement and safer gathering during high-water conditions.

03 / SHARE

Concentrate collective services

Workshops, markets, shared rooms and neighbourhood facilities turn the intervention into an everyday civic resource rather than an isolated emergency facility.

05 / Modular construction

A system assembled together.

Rather than treating the dwelling as a single finished object, the proposal separates it into three legible layers: a lightweight enclosure, a primary structural frame, and a kit of environmental systems. The exploded axonometric shows how these elements could be assembled independently, repaired over time and adapted to different neighbourhood needs.

01 / Envelope

Replaceable layers for everyday life.

Bamboo wall and flooring panels, a wooden ceiling and screened openings define the inhabited spaces without becoming the main load-bearing system. This distinction makes ventilation, shading and component replacement part of the architectural logic rather than afterthoughts.

02 / Structure

A raised frame with identifiable connections.

Columns, primary beams and joint connectors establish a shared support grid, while the diagram proposes telescopic concrete foundations below the occupied deck. Their dimensions, flood resistance and suitability beside the railway would still require site-specific engineering.

03 / Climate and water

Environmental services as accessible parts.

Rainwater gutters, solar panels, the proposed HydroHarvest system, sediment retention and storage units are shown as distinct assemblies. Keeping these systems visible and serviceable offers a basis for maintenance and phased installation; the drawing does not establish verified treatment performance.

02 / Collective construction

Building is a shared process.

The six-panel sequence begins with prefabricated components being delivered to the site, followed by community design consultation. Ground anchoring and foundation preparation precede cooperative frame assembly; the final stages introduce ventilation and HydroHarvest equipment, then rooftop solar power.

From delivery to occupation

Sequencing allows participation at different scales.

The illustrations connect specialist work, local decision-making and straightforward assembly tasks. Prefabrication may limit prolonged disruption on a constrained riverbank, while a consistent structural kit could allow components to be installed or replaced in phases.

Collective agency

Consultation comes before construction.

Participation is not simply a source of labour. Decisions about layout, shared uses, maintenance and access would need to be negotiated with residents before any construction sequence is fixed. Training, safe installation, financing and long-term stewardship remain essential questions for further development.
