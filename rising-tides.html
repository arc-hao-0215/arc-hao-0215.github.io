Rising Tides, Resilient Lives

Water-Resilient Community Design

Kampung Melayu, JakartaFlood Resilience · Civic Infrastructure · Collective Life2025

SOM China Fellowship · Winner

How can flood-prone infrastructure become a civic sanctuary?

Design approach

Rising Tides, Resilient Lives organises a public spine beneath the Ciliwung River viaduct at Kampung Melayu. Workshops, markets, kitchens, shared rooms, wetland edges and modular dwellings form a connected architectural system. Its principal design operation is sectional: everyday activity occupies a shaded civic landscape, while elevated access and adaptable rooms support a different pattern of use during inundation. The proposal uses the viaduct as a spatial reference; connections to transport infrastructure and structural feasibility are not assumed.

SOM China Fellowship — Winner

01

One structure. Two seasons.

The two sections keep the main architectural frame comparable while changing the relation between water, circulation and programme. In ordinary conditions, the shared spaces support exchange, food preparation and work. In the flood scenario, access shifts toward elevated routes and boat landings, while parts of the civic programme accommodate refuge and relief.

This is a design for connected services rather than a collection of independently raised objects. A refuge needs a route to supplies and sanitation; a landing needs a clear relationship to occupied rooms. The comparison below makes those adjacencies visible. It is a spatial scenario, not a simulation of a verified flood level or a completed emergency operating plan.

Seasonal system
02

Water as a collaborator.

Wetland buffers and water-related infrastructure are placed beside daily civic uses. Roof-water collection, wastewater treatment and overflow are read as distinct parts of the proposed system. Their separation matters: the presence of planted treatment space does not establish that floodwater is clean or that every flow can be reused.

The landscape helps organise edges between occupation and changing water. The design draws circulation, communal rooms and environmental systems together so their conflicts can be examined. Treatment capacity, water quality, debris, service isolation and safe levels remain subjects for engineering development.

03

A system assembled together.

The exploded dwelling study separates the frame, enclosure, ventilation and environmental components. Prefabrication is proposed as a way to make assembly legible and components replaceable. Community consultation and cooperative construction belong to the intended delivery approach; the unbuilt proposal does not establish that these processes have occurred.

At the larger scale, repetition allows individual units to relate to the shared spine. The architectural emphasis is on the connection between a dwelling and collective access, rather than the unit in isolation. Material availability, repair skills, procurement and the allocation of maintenance responsibilities would determine how this assembly strategy could be realised.

Scope & development

The companion research tests adaptation, unequal capacity and recovery after flooding. This project page concentrates on the selected spatial response: seasonal sections, programme relationships and a modular assembly system.
