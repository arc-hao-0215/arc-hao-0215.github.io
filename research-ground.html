Research / Essay 02

Ground as an archive.

Memory / LandscapePenghu, Taiwan · 2024Design research

View related project ↗

Abstract

A former coastal battery contains more than structures to be preserved. Its trenches, changes of level, and relationship to the sea form a spatial record of defence. This enquiry asks how a museum can make that record legible while opening the site to new cultural life.

Research question

How can architectural intervention reveal an inherited landscape without reducing it to a backdrop?

Method

The study reads historical and natural layers alongside plans, sections, and a sequence of spatial views. Existing bunkers, trenches, and artillery positions provide reference points for a route. Sectional design examines how buried galleries, open courts, and coastal views can connect inherited ground with new occupation.

Preservation as a relationship

An isolated remnant can communicate age, yet lose the spatial relationships that made it meaningful. Here, the enquiry extends from individual military structures to the ground between them. A trench is both an inherited trace and a possible approach; the contour is both a physical constraint and an organiser of movement. Reuse begins by asking what these relationships allow a visitor to understand.

Sequence as a way of reading

The route moves between compression and release: protected passage, darker gallery, open courtyard, light-filled chamber, and sea. This sequence proposes a way of encountering history through the body. The museum does not rely on a single monumental image. Its meaning develops through duration, changing light, and the alternation between enclosed space and an exposed horizon.

An incomplete archive

New galleries slip beneath the ground while existing structures remain participants in the route. The proposition is not to restore a single historical moment, but to make different layers available to contemporary use. The portfolio records a design interpretation of the site; claims about visitor experience or conservation performance would require further investigation.
