Their Story

Community Calendar & Editorial Design

Eastern District, Hong KongCommunity · Cultural Memory · Storytelling2024

Field Research Sponsored by Swire Properties

How can everyday stories become a shared cultural record?

Design approach

Their Story translates an Eastern District community enquiry into a printed calendar. Its design medium is the edited sequence: local stories and images enter an object encountered repeatedly through the year. The project extends architectural attention to cultural representation—how a neighbourhood is described, remembered and made available to shared attention.

Context

Developed during the Swire Properties Bi-city Youth Cultural Leadership Programme, this work sits alongside comparative study of urban placemaking and cultural ecosystems in Beijing and Hong Kong.

The calendar as a repeated encounter

A calendar is read differently from an exhibition catalogue. It remains in an everyday setting and is returned to over time. The project uses that duration to give community stories a recurring presence, rather than treating them as material for a single event.

The design output brings research into a finite editorial form. Selection, ordering and the relationship between image and text become the principal decisions. The page presents that cultural object and its context without claiming that it represents every resident or resolves the neighbourhood’s differing interests.

A scale between the personal and the district

The project keeps the scale of individual experience in view within a larger account of Eastern District. Its connection to architecture lies in attention to the people and practices through which a place acquires meaning, as well as the buildings through which it is usually identified.

The accompanying Hong Kong essay develops the wider questions about developers, cultural institutions and stewardship. The calendar remains a distinct output: an act of editing and circulation through which specific stories can be encountered.
