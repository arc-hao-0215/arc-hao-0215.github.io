An infrastructure of care.

Abstract

A pilgrimage route is simultaneously a cultural sequence, a demanding journey, and an ecologically vulnerable landscape. Around Mount Kailash, the question of intervention is also a question of restraint: where can architecture provide care, and where should it recede?

Research question

How can essential shelter and services support a sacred journey while respecting its landscape and ritual sequence?

Method

Territorial mapping and research into ecological and religious vulnerability guide the placement and character of interventions. GIS-based study is read together with the experience of the route. The portfolio develops three responses—a courtyard lodge, compact supply stations, and an exhibition hall—to examine different forms of rest, exposure, and interpretation.

The route precedes the building

The enquiry begins with movement through a landscape rather than a freestanding programme. Needs change along the journey, as do the cultural meanings of particular places. This makes location, visibility, and the scale of an intervention part of the research question. A building is considered through its relationship to a longer sequence, not only through its own plan.

Different forms of care

At the First Tavern, the existing footprint becomes the basis for a courtyard lodge that supports rest and shared space. Supply stations explore compact, insulated shelter and sanitation with reduced ground disturbance. The exhibition hall is set away from the ritual ground, using its position and material character to approach interpretation with restraint. These are related responses, but they are not interchangeable.

Restraint as an architectural criterion

The study uses the provision of essential services to test how much architecture is needed. Retaining footprints, concentrating facilities, and protecting views become ways to limit the intervention’s presence. The proposal remains a design study: environmental performance and cultural acceptance cannot be inferred from drawings alone. Those questions would need continued engagement and site-specific assessment.
