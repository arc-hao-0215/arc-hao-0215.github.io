The Condenser

Theater & Civic Space Design

Wudaokou, BeijingPerformance · Social Infrastructure · Public Life2025

Can a theater be a civic space before and after the performance?

Design approach

The Condenser gives a theatre two connected lives: enclosed rooms for performance and a more loosely occupied civic interior. Located among Wudaokou’s university, workplace and residential settings, it receives public approaches from the southwest and northwest, opens the ground, and carries shared space upward through foyers and stairs to a rooftop garden and amphitheatre. The blue and orange performance volumes remain identifiable within a translucent envelope. The project is an unbuilt academic proposal developed in spring 2025.

01

A public ground. A vertical gathering place.

The ground is opened to support passage and arrival from two principal directions. At the southwest corner, a cut in the volume and a reflective surface give the entrance a distinct presence. The approach is intended to work as part of the surrounding pedestrian setting, with places to pause before entering the performance sequence.

The long section shows how the public ground relates to enclosed halls and upper shared spaces. The proposal concentrates performance in recognisable volumes and uses the spaces between them to extend occupation. Audience movement and backstage/loading access remain differentiated, allowing the public character of the building to be developed without assuming that its working areas are unrestricted.

02

The foyer as a social condenser.

Stairs are the main device connecting the civic interior. Landings and foyers become places for waiting, conversation and informal viewing. Between levels three and four, the larger gathering stair is conceived as a forum for talks, screenings and unscheduled occupation. Its scale distinguishes it from stairs used primarily for movement.

The design places opportunities to stay alongside the route through the building. This creates a sequence of changing pace rather than a single foyer emptied into the auditorium. The next technical stage would have to maintain accessible parallel routes, clear egress and acoustic separation when gathering and movement occur at the same time.

03

Distinct rooms, one civic frame.

The blue western theatre and orange southern theatre respond to the different urban conditions around the site. A translucent polycarbonate envelope allows their interior light and occupation to register after dark. Colour differentiates the performance rooms within a common architectural frame; it does not replace the need for legible entrances and boundaries.

At roof level, the garden and open-air amphitheatre extend the range of settings for performance and gathering. The programme axonometric clarifies how these spaces relate to the enclosed rooms and backstage system below. The design is evaluated through these relationships, while opening hours, staffing, weather protection, acoustic performance and technical stage requirements remain part of further development.
