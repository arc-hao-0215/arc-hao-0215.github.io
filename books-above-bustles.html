Books Above
Bustles

BeijingCivic architecture2024

Can a library reconnect a city?

Design approach

Books Above Bustles arranges library and shared civic programmes in suspended volumes between Chang’an Avenue and the hutongs. Gardens, bridges and reading spaces connect the volumes while the ground remains available for a more permeable relationship with the city. A mega-truss and modular timber grid organise the structural proposition. The section brings this support together with movement, programme and environmental systems so their relationships can be read within one architectural composition.

01

Read the building through its systems.

The sectional perspective is presented through four readings. Space locates the public routes and places to remain; structure identifies the supporting framework; climate places the proposed envelope, roof and ventilation systems; programme distinguishes reading, learning, work and shared rooms. The coloured overlays are interpretive guides to the drawing, not technical simulation outputs.

The design concentrates on connections between these systems. Bridges relate occupied volumes, gardens introduce another scale of shared interior, and the ground provides a civic setting beneath the institution. These relationships give the library a vertical public sequence while leaving questions of acoustic separation, servicing and accessible routes for detailed testing.

A vertical public realm

Gardens, bridges, reading spaces, and an open ground floor connect the building’s compartments into a continuous civic interior.

02

A civic room above the city.

Suspension makes the ground a central architectural decision. The building’s mass is held above a more open pedestrian landscape, changing how the large avenue and smaller urban fabric can meet. The elevation and reflected surroundings give the volumes an urban presence, while the spaces beneath and between them carry the public-space proposition.

That ground should be read as part of an operating institution. The design provides a spatial framework for permeability; opening hours, entry control, maintenance and security would determine its realised public role. The project does not assume that visual transparency alone establishes unrestricted access.

03

Testing the spatial proposition.

Physical models study the proximity of the volumes, the depth of openings and the continuity of the supporting frame. They provide a second way to examine what can be difficult to read in a complex sectional perspective: where light enters, how voids align and how the mass is experienced from below.

The models are spatial studies. Long-span structural behaviour, deflection, connections, fire strategy and environmental performance require separate technical development. Their contribution at this stage is to make the relationship between built volume and shared void open to comparison and revision.

Scope & development

The Public Floor, the related research essay, examines thresholds, access and the institutional meaning of common ground. Here, the emphasis is the proposed massing, structural order, sectional connections and model studies.
