Books Above Bustles

Urban Library Design

Xidan, BeijingKnowledge · Civic Life · InfrastructureAcademic Studio
Project proposition

A library that is also a route through the city.

At Xidan, the monumental scale of West Chang’an Avenue meets a finer-grained neighbourhood and a busy subway interchange. A civic building at this threshold could do more than occupy a closed block: it could offer the city a new public ground.

Books Above Bustles imagines a layered library where a lifted institutional volume frames a landscape of shared rooms. Reading, learning, workspaces, gardens and gathering spaces connect through a single spatial sequence.

01 / Urban context

Between two scales of the city.

West Chang’an Avenue, Xidan’s smaller northern streets and the subway occupy very different urban scales. Their meeting point offers a chance to turn an inward-looking institutional site into a more generous threshold.

This is the project’s proposed site plan; the landscape and building placement are part of the design rather than a survey of existing built conditions.

02 / Site issue

An interrupted public ground.

The issue is not only accommodating a library, but negotiating constrained pedestrian movement, separated programmes and the distance between the main avenue and the neighbouring streets.

What if the building could reconnect its edges?

A conventional closed block would reinforce separation. Instead, the proposal lifts major programmes, introduces a shared ground and uses a large atrium to link spaces that would otherwise remain isolated.

01 / URBAN SCALE

Two different street worlds

The monumental avenue and fine-grained neighbourhood call for different kinds of civic access.

02 / PEDESTRIAN ACCESS

Interrupted movement

Roads, enclosed blocks and transit levels constrain routes through the neighbourhood.

03 / PUBLIC LIFE

More than one function

Reading, education and gathering could share a connected civic space.

03 / Design approach

Four operations, one public continuum.

The strategy turns a seemingly singular volume into a sequence of accessible territories. Each operation clarifies a different aspect of the relationship between city, programme and environment.

01 / FLOAT

Open the ground

Lift major volumes to create sheltered public space and long urban views.

02 / OPEN

Light the centre

Cut an atrium through the building to connect programmes across levels.

03 / LINK

Bridge the uses

Terraces, bridges and stairs link reading, learning and collective spaces.

04 / MODULATE

Shape a boundary

Explore a layered screen and roof landscape as environmental intentions.

04 / Architecture

A vertical public realm.

The project comes together most clearly in section: a shared ground, layered civic rooms and an inhabitable void establish a continuous interior rather than isolated storeys.

A / Organizing section

Multiple grounds within one building.

From the metro and public entrance to education, books, office spaces and the roof garden, the drawing explains how different public territories meet vertically.

B / Plans and occupation

Programmes arranged around a shared void.

The plans give different uses a degree of independence while keeping their relationship to the atrium, shared circulation and urban ground legible.

C / Circulation & programme

Moving through the library, not simply entering it.

The exploded axonometric expresses the programme as a sequence: transit and urban ground, commerce, education, reading, gathering, dining and workspaces.

Circulation becomes an architectural experience—an opportunity to pause, look across the atrium and encounter other activities.

  • Urban ground / public arrival
  • Education and reading / connected terraces
  • Garden and gathering / shared interior life
  • Workspaces / quieter upper levels
05 / Spatial experience

More than a place to read.

Bookshelves form inhabitable edges. Tiered floors, open bridges and generous interiors support performances, exhibitions and informal gathering alongside everyday reading.

A / Arrival as civic interior

The threshold to the subway becomes a room.

A broad stepped space brings the movement of commuters into contact with book-lined terraces, reading and public gathering above.

B / Collective spaces

A library for both quiet and shared activities.

The central volume holds contrasting rhythms: reading on its edges, cultural programmes and collective events in the middle.

C / Urban presence

A clear civic figure above an open ground.

From West Chang’an Avenue, the screen gives the library a coherent presence while the open lower level shows its intended relation to public life on the street.

06 / Envelope & systems

A boundary between city and interior life.

Technical studies consider how the external screen could modulate appearance and relate to the structural framework and warm-toned library interior.

Envelope / technical investigation

Transparent, translucent, reflective.

Three possible screen conditions explore changing visibility and reflection. The accompanying details test an exterior enclosure, interior bookshelf walls and their relationship to the structural frame.

These are conceptual and assembly studies, not verified operational systems. Facade control, structure, climate performance, fire safety and maintenance require further coordination.

07 / Physical models

Making volume and void tangible.

Physical models test the difference between a unified reflective enclosure and an illuminated, inhabited public interior.

Scope & development

This academic design proposal studies public circulation, programme, the urban ground, spatial section, envelope and structural expression. Detailed engineering, structural deflection, facade mechanisms, acoustic separation, fire strategy, access and maintenance would need further validation before construction.
