Books Above Bustles

Urban Library Design

Chang’an Avenue, BeijingKnowledge · Civic Life · Infrastructure2024

A library that is also a route through the city. Books Above Bustles imagines the former single-purpose institutional block as a permeable civic structure: a sheltered ground, a sequence of stacked public rooms, and a new shared interior above one of Beijing’s busiest avenues.

Can a library reconnect a city?

Design approach

Between the monumental scale of West Chang’an Avenue and the smaller grain of the streets to its north, Books Above Bustles replaces a single closed block with a layered public proposition. Suspended reading and work volumes frame a ground-level landscape linked to the metro. An atrium, shared terraces, bridges and gardens gather different civic programmes around a continuous route.

01

The city at the threshold.

A broad avenue, a busy subway interchange and a fine-grain neighbourhood meet at this site. Rather than treating the library as an isolated destination, the proposal tests whether its ground plane can become a connector between urban scales.

The problem

A closed block at the meeting of two cities.

The existing condition separates neighbourhood movement from the monumental street and interrupts a potential public route. The design opens this threshold by lifting major programmes, allowing a landscaped ground and a shared interior to extend toward the subway.

02

Four moves, one continuous public route.

Floating volumes liberate the ground; an opening brings daylight into the stack; bridges reconnect separate programmes; and a porous outer screen and planted roof propose more responsive environmental boundaries.

Programme / movement

The exploded axonometric turns an apparent single volume into a sequence of distinct public environments: commerce at the base, education and collective rooms, reading terraces, garden and dining, and quieter offices above.

The vertical route is the organising idea. It links programmes that would otherwise remain separate and offers multiple places to pause and look across the atrium.

03

One building, several grounds.

The site landscape, elevated floors and roof garden operate as related public territories. From the avenue the building appears as a clear hovering figure; in plan, the large central opening negotiates between enclosure and open space.

04

A civic interior for more than reading.

The tall atrium is conceived as an urban living room. Stacks become architectural edges, terraces double as gathering and performance seating, and daylight connects otherwise separated interior levels.

Long section / 1: conceptual study

The sectional perspective gives the most complete account of the proposition. From the below-grade metro connection and public ground to education rooms, stacked books, bridges, offices and the planted roof, a single view describes how the civic interior is inhabited vertically.

Labels for ventilation, the mirror façade, timber grid, structural spans and roof systems indicate intended relationships; their performance remains subject to technical design and verification.

05

An envelope that changes how the city is seen.

The external screen is conceived as an adjustable optical and climatic layer rather than a fixed transparent wall. Material and assembly drawings begin to test how this exterior, the interior shelves and the structural frame meet.

Envelope / tectonics

Three proposed façade states—transparent, translucent and mirror—alter views, reflection and the perceptual depth of the building. Interior and exterior details locate the screen beside the steel truss and floor structure, while warm-toned bookshelves and linings establish a different scale of material experience inside.

These drawings express a design intention rather than verified operational switching, thermal or daylight performance.

06

Testing volume, void and light.

Physical models make the contrast between the reflective outer volume and the illuminated civic interior tangible. They also help evaluate how the elevated mass is perceived from the surrounding city and the open ground below.

The proposal is a spatial study. Structural deflection, façade mechanisms, fire strategy, acoustic separation, maintenance and access would need further coordination before the architecture could be realised.

Scope & development

The design work explores civic circulation, layered programme, public ground, envelope and structural expression. For a separate examination of access, public thresholds and the institutional meaning of common ground, see the related research essay.
