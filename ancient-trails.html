Lightly in the Forest

Eco-Resort Design

Gaoligong Mountains, YunnanTea Heritage · Ecology · Low-Impact Architecture2026

Outstanding Graduation Thesis · Tsinghua University

How can architecture inhabit a working mountain landscape without overwhelming it?

Design approach

The proposal imagines an eco-retreat at Fenglaoye Ancient Tea Garden, where tea cultivation, ancient routes and forest ecology remain part of everyday life. Instead of one dominant building, a network of shared facilities, lightweight accommodation and places to pause is arranged along existing ground conditions. The architectural vocabulary shifts between timber decks, planted thresholds and carefully placed stone enclosures, while construction is studied from the journey of a material to its smallest connection.

01 / Territory

A trail before a building.

The Tea Horse Trail provides a larger cultural and geographical structure. The project translates that linear relationship into a smaller network of arrivals, gathering spaces, accommodation and outdoor activities at the tea farm.

02 / Sequence

The route is a series of thresholds.

Circulation is also a place to sit, watch and meet. Platforms, bamboo screens and stone walls create different degrees of enclosure without treating every outdoor space as a conventional interior.

03 / Dwelling

Rooms for staying. Places for slowing down.

A lightweight cabin approaches the forest through an openable edge, shading and a raised floor. The bathing area uses stone and the existing view as a quieter, more enclosed counterpart.

04 / Assembly

A building conceived as parts.

The component study separates timber structure, roofing, screen panels, fittings, decks and point foundations. The intention is a system that can be assembled and maintained with limited disturbance—not a claim that its performance has already been verified.

Scope & development

This graduation design proposal studies spatial distribution, walking networks and component-level construction in a working tea landscape. Environmental performance, site ecology, structural engineering, construction access and long-term operations remain subjects for subsequent verification.
