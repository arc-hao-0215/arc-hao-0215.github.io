Lightly in the Forest

Eco-Resort Design

Gaoligong Mountains, YunnanTea Heritage · Ecology · Low-Impact Architecture2026

Outstanding Graduation Thesis · Tsinghua University

Can a trail reconnect the livelihoods and landscapes it once sustained?

Design approach

At Fenglaoye Ancient Tea Garden, the proposal reconnects a tea factory, existing accommodation and a dispersed mountain landscape through a walking network. Reception, production, rest and bathing are assigned different degrees of architectural presence. More collective uses concentrate near accessible ground; smaller accommodation units occupy selected clearings. The design works from the relation between route and programme down to the assembly of a raised floor. It is a graduation design proposal, with environmental and operational performance still to be tested.

Tsinghua Outstanding Graduation Thesis

01

A network before an object.

The layout begins with the places that already anchor the site. A public reception establishes arrival and orientation, while the route reconnects tea-making with the garden and stopping places. The intervention is distributed so that movement reveals a sequence of activities rather than an isolated destination.

Public and private movement are differentiated within that network. Collective facilities need to be easy to find and service; accommodation needs a degree of separation from the walking route. Changes in alignment and threshold allow visitors to remain connected to the landscape without making every room part of a public promenade. The network drawing should be read through these branches and relationships.

02

Different ground. Different degrees of intervention.

Construction changes with the ground. The studies use raised floors, point foundations, timber members and bracing to organise small units without a continuous ground slab. Modular components and replaceable connections are proposed to make assembly and later repair more manageable along the route.

The architectural detail is tied to logistics. A compact footprint still requires a way to bring materials in, install foundations and maintain services. The proposal therefore concentrates more demanding shared uses where access is less constrained. Root zones, slope stability, installation methods and the treatment cycle for bathing facilities remain technical questions for a subsequent stage.
