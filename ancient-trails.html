Lightly in the Forest

Eco-Resort Design

Gaoligong Mountains, YunnanTea Heritage · Ecology · Low-Impact ArchitectureGraduation Design

Outstanding Graduation Thesis · Tsinghua University

Project proposition

A retreat that follows the forest, rather than replacing it.

At Fenglaoye Ancient Tea Garden, the landscape is already occupied by tea cultivation, historic routes, forest and everyday movement. Lightly in the Forest asks how a new form of dwelling can join this working mountain without becoming its dominant presence.

The proposal distributes small places to arrive, meet, rest and stay along a network of trails. Public programmes gather closer to existing facilities; lighter structures occupy selected forest clearings. From the site plan to the timber connection, architecture is treated as a careful addition rather than a replacement.

01 / Context

A working mountain, not an empty site.

The Tea Horse Trail connects tea gardens, a factory, Dahaoping Village and cultural landmarks across the Gaoligong foothills. The site belongs to this larger landscape of production, movement and memory before it belongs to any architectural proposal.

02 / Site issue

How can new occupation avoid overwhelming what is already there?

The design challenge is not simply where to put accommodation. It is how to support visitors while maintaining a working landscape, negotiating steep terrain and limiting the physical reach of construction.

More visitors need not mean one larger footprint.

Building a conventional, consolidated resort would risk treating paths, slope and canopy as vacant land to be reorganized. The project instead starts from the existing spatial relationships and asks what the mountain can accommodate with fewer, more deliberate interventions.

01 / CULTURE

Production before tourism

Tea cultivation, the village and inherited paths give the site an active life beyond the visitor programme.

02 / TERRAIN

Topography as a constraint

Sloping ground and tree cover argue for careful placement rather than extensive grading and continuous foundations.

03 / ACCESS

Connection without saturation

Different degrees of public access and privacy need a legible network, not a uniform spread of buildings.

03 / Design approach

A network before an object.

The architectural response begins at the landscape scale. Routes define a sequence of collective, shared and private places; buildings form clusters and smaller stops rather than a singular destination.

01 / CONCENTRATE

Gather collective uses

Arrival, reception and workshop programmes remain closer to the accessible public core.

02 / CONNECT

Work with the trail

Junctions, viewing points and short pauses give the route an architectural rhythm.

03 / DISPERSE

Place buildings in clearings

Smaller accommodation clusters negotiate privacy, trees and terrain at a more intimate scale.

Reading the master plan

From the shared edge to the forest interior.

The plan brings the broad strategy down to individual pieces of architecture. Access is organized as a changing sequence, with the most collective uses near arrival and quieter programmes further along the paths.

  1. 01
    Arrive and gather. Reception and tea-related facilities anchor the more accessible portion of the site.
  2. 02
    Move and pause. Junction decks, shelters and viewing platforms punctuate the walking network.
  3. 03
    Stay and withdraw. Cabins and bathing areas occupy selected locations within the tree canopy.
04 / Architecture

Walking, pausing, dwelling.

Rather than one repeated architectural object, the project proposes different relationships with the ground: a more collective arrival, open thresholds along the trail and intimate spaces within the forest.

A / The shared ground

An arrival shaped by the slope.

The reception pavilion brings collective programmes together at an accessible point. Its sectional relationship to the hillside introduces a gradual transition from infrastructure to landscape.

B / Along the trail

The route becomes a place.

Small material shifts change how visitors move and stop. Timber creates an open, inhabited platform; stone gathers and frames a moment along the path.

C / Within the forest

More intimate forms of inhabitation.

Accommodation and bathing provide contrasting atmospheres: a lightweight room with an open edge and a more enclosed stone setting facing the mountain.

05 / Construction

Built as parts, not permanent ground.

At the smallest scale, the proposal studies dry assembly, timber and bamboo elements, movable screens and individual footings. The aim is to keep construction legible, maintainable and potentially reversible.

Physical model / spatial test

A small presence in a larger landscape.

The model tests how elevated cabins register against the wooded hillside. Construction feasibility, slope stability, tree-root protection, environmental performance and long-term operation remain questions for further technical verification.

Scope & development

This graduation design is a spatial and architectural proposal, not a completed building. It studies a distributed programme, walking network and component-based construction. Site ecology, structure, access and operational performance would need further assessment before implementation.
