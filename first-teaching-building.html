First Teaching Building Renewal

University Classroom Renovation

Tsinghua University, BeijingEducation · Adaptive Reuse · Interior ArchitectureThird-floor renewal · 682 m²

Commissioned by Tsinghua University

Design premise

Keeping the building, changing the ways it is used.

Built in 1952, Tsinghua University's First Teaching Building has accommodated successive ways of teaching, learning, and exchanging knowledge. Its restrained modernist structure — the measured arrangement of rooms, windows, and circulation — provides a durable architectural framework.

Rather than replacing that inherited order, this third-floor renewal works through small, coordinated interventions: acoustics, lighting, building services, material finishes, and furnishings. The aim is to make room for new patterns of use while allowing the original building to remain legible.

01 / Historical continuity

A building in continuity.

The 1952 drawings reveal an architectural order defined by symmetrical teaching wings, clear circulation, generous openings, and a plain yet precise language of construction. This historical framework is the point of departure, not an obstacle to renovation.

Original documentation / 1952

Read the building before intervening.

The historical plan and elevation are presented together to retain the relationship between the building's spatial organisation and its institutional expression. Their proportions guide the scale and location of later changes.

The renewed third floor therefore retains the inherited structural frame and existing window openings, concentrating new work on surfaces, services, equipment, and interior occupation.

02 / Existing condition

The limits of a fixed classroom.

The existing teaching room is organised around a singular direction of attention: rows of fixed desks face the blackboard, with equipment and circulation subordinated to lectures. The room remains a valued part of the building, but its configuration offers limited support for smaller exchanges and varied teaching formats.

Before / Teaching room

From one way of teaching to many.

The existing stepped seating establishes a strong front-to-back hierarchy. Together with aging interior surfaces and layered building services, it illustrates the tension between a durable architectural shell and changing patterns of teaching.

The proposal does not seek to erase this history. Instead, it uses the remaining structure, windows, doors, and teaching-room proportions as the basis for more versatile interior environments.

03 / Spatial strategy

Small changes, new possibilities.

Across 682 m² of the third floor, the intervention works within the building's existing footprint. Two large classrooms remain at either end; a multifunctional classroom, circulation space and teachers' lounge extend the range of learning and shared use.

01 / Preserve

Retain the inherited order.

Keep the structural framework, original proportions, window openings and existing timber doors visibly part of the experience.

02 / Integrate

Make technology unobtrusive.

Coordinate acoustic surfaces, lighting, air supply and return, heating covers and teaching equipment with the established architectural rhythm.

03 / Adapt

Allow everyday use to evolve.

Introduce adaptable furniture, informal seating and spaces that support discussion, presentation, screening and work outside scheduled classes.

Intervention diagram / Third floor

Work within the inherited plan.

The annotated drawing identifies where the third-floor teaching rooms and circulation can be adjusted within the existing structural and window rhythm. Rather than restructuring the building, the scheme concentrates change in the interior fit-out and how its rooms are used.

The two large classrooms continue to anchor the ends of the plan. Between them, targeted alterations support more varied teaching, shared circulation and informal exchange.

Renewal plan / 682 m²

One floor, several scales of learning.

The proposed plan keeps two 96-seat classrooms of 174.3 m² each while introducing a 121.6 m² multifunctional teaching space. A 184.4 m² circulation zone becomes a shared learning threshold rather than only a passage.

A 27.4 m² teachers' lounge completes the sequence with a smaller place for preparation and conversation. These spaces are designed to coexist without concealing the logic of the 1952 building.

04 / Informal learning

From corridor to common ground.

Circulation is recast as more than movement between scheduled classes. Small work surfaces, places to sit, and power points invite temporary occupation — waiting, reading, working and conversations between students and teachers.

Circulation / 184.4 m²

An everyday threshold between learning and exchange.

The corridor's narrow, linear character is kept. Rather than broadening it through major alteration, the design establishes a quieter rhythm of places to pause along the edges, leaving a clear path through the centre.

Existing mahogany swing doors remain recognizable, while white diffuse flooring, timber-toned floor strips and pale linen-coloured walls establish continuity between new and old. Power sockets support short work sessions; heating and ventilation are integrated with wall and ceiling details.

05 / Teaching environments

Spaces for different ways of learning.

Within unchanged classroom boundaries, updated acoustic, visual and technical infrastructure supports contemporary teaching formats. Familiar spatial proportions are retained; the capacity for different forms of occupation is expanded.

Large classrooms / 2 × 174.3 m²

The lecture room, newly equipped.

The two 96-seat rooms retain their large window openings and original timber doors. Timber wall linings and acoustic wood-wool ceilings soften the atmosphere and support sound performance without obscuring the established structural rhythm.

Suspended displays and presentation equipment serve lecture and discussion settings, while coordinated lights and air outlets reduce the visual accumulation of services. Wood-toned flooring and joinery create a consistent interior language across the rooms.

Multifunctional classroom / 121.6 m²

One room, several ways of gathering.

Instead of prescribing a single seating arrangement, the multifunctional classroom relies on loose, adaptable furniture and shared presentation surfaces. It can accommodate lectures, project reviews, screenings, workshops and student activities within the existing room envelope.

The flexibility is deliberately modest: the room's architecture remains stable while furniture, media and modes of occupation change.

06 / Everyday life and materiality

A place to pause and exchange.

The 27.4 m² teachers' lounge offers a smaller register of communal life: preparing for class, taking a break, or meeting informally. Its scale complements the classrooms and corridor, extending the renewal beyond scheduled instruction.

Teachers' lounge / 27.4 m²

Supporting the time between classes.

Small tables and a shared writing wall form a setting for preparation and informal exchange. Daylight from retained windows reaches a restrained palette of white surfaces and timber-lined lower walls.

Material intervention remains deliberately quiet. The renewed interiors draw on wood veneer, pale linen-like wall finishes and coordinated acoustic ceilings, retaining the legibility of historic windows and doors while concealing the complexity of new building services.

Continuity through change

A modest intervention with a longer life.

From a 1952 public teaching building to an environment for contemporary collective learning, the project's ambition is not a new architectural identity. It is to refine what is already there: to carry an enduring spatial order forward by adapting the details through which people use it every day.
