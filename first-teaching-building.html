First Teaching Building Renewal

University Classroom Renovation

Tsinghua University, BeijingEducation · Adaptive Reuse · Interior Architecture2025–2026

Commissioned by Tsinghua University

How can small changes renew an enduring place of learning?

Design approach

This commission renews the third floor of Tsinghua’s First Teaching Building through restrained changes to rooms, surfaces and building services. The 30 January 2026 proposal covers 682 m²: two 96-seat classrooms, a multifunctional classroom, circulation and a teachers’ lounge. The design retains the inherited structure, proportions and window openings. Its main work is coordination—making acoustic treatment, lighting, ventilation, equipment and furniture support contemporary teaching within the building’s existing order. The images show a design stage, not completed construction.

01

Renew the room; retain its order.

The two large classrooms remain the principal teaching rooms, while the multifunctional room provides another scale of occupation between them. The plan places their entrances along the shared circulation spine and retains a separate teachers’ lounge. The programme combines fixed-capacity teaching with a room that can change between lecture, review, screening and group activity.

The original building’s 1952 order guides the extent of alteration. Structure and openings remain the primary frame; new work is concentrated in finishes, equipment and the organisation of use. Keeping this hierarchy clear helps distinguish the inherited building from elements expected to change again as teaching methods evolve.

02

An inhabited corridor.

The corridor is treated as an occupied part of the floor. Seating and power points support short work sessions and conversations between classes. Retained timber doors remain visible within a restrained palette of pale wall surfaces and differentiated flooring. The design seeks continuity through material and alignment rather than a new decorative identity.

Wood-wool acoustic panels follow the ceiling order. Lighting and supply/return air outlets are coordinated at their edges, while heating is integrated with the lower wall and grilles. These details bring services into the same spatial composition as the doors, seating and circulation. Final service sizing, acoustic performance and maintenance access require coordination during detailed design.

03

Different ways to learn.

The classrooms combine teaching equipment with layouts suited to their different capacities. The multifunctional room uses adaptable furniture and presentation surfaces to accommodate several arrangements. Its flexibility comes from changing occupation within an established room, rather than claiming that every part of the plan can serve every activity.

The teachers’ lounge is a smaller setting for preparation, pause and conversation. Together with the corridor seating, it extends the commission beyond the scheduled lesson. The design balances these shared uses with clear circulation, equipment integration and the need for teaching rooms to operate without unnecessary disturbance.
