Scaffold of Care

An Infrastructure of Coexistence

Zhongli, TaiwanMixed-Use Civic ArchitectureMigration · Public Space · Care2025
RecognitionFeatured in ArchDaily Student Project Awards 2025 ↗
The proposition

A place to stay, not only to pass through.

Around Zhongli Station, public space serves a city shaped by migration yet does not always offer its migrant communities somewhere to gather without having to buy, move along or explain their presence.

Scaffold of Care transforms an overlooked gap on Changjiang Road into a shared urban living room. A familiar temporary construction system becomes the frame for enduring relationships, services and cultural life.

01

The living room deficit.

Near the station, commercial streets, informal gathering places and the river sit close together yet remain socially and spatially fragmented. The first drawing records different communities, faiths and practices in the same urban field rather than reducing the area to an empty site.

Reading the site

Visibility is not the same as belonging.

The railway station and surrounding shopping district become meeting points, especially on days off. Informal sitting, eating and socialising already make an urban commons—but these activities often lack space designed for comfort or legitimacy.

The proposal also identifies a potential connection between Changjiang Road and the Laojie River, where urban access, ecological edges and public gathering could be considered together.

02

Reclaiming an urban gap.

Rather than a sealed community building, the proposal establishes a recognisable civic address in the fine-grain urban fabric. Its porous scaffold acts as a new public threshold—open to the street, adaptable in programme and legible from different approach routes.

Urban proposition

Temporary structure, lasting public claim.

The lightweight frame is deliberately distinct from the surrounding permanent blocks. It gives everyday public activities a visible place without proposing a monument to any one community.

The intervention is conceived as a civic plug-in: small in footprint, generous in encounters and able to accommodate different schedules of use.

03

An open framework for care.

Movement passes beneath and through the scaffold, then rises to terraces around the shared interior. Rather than assigning one floor to one identity, the section allows markets, learning, service and gathering to meet across several levels.

04

A kit of shared spaces.

The system is composed from reconfigurable scaffold platforms and lightweight enclosures. The architectural unit can become a classroom, planted terrace, communal table or sheltered social space; the project treats adaptation as a condition of public use, not merely a construction technique.

Programmes of care

Everyday support, not a single-purpose institution.

A cross-cultural kitchen and shared meals provide opportunities for exchange. Community classrooms and co-learning activities extend the building's use beyond festivals. The brief also imagines NGO-supported advice and legal consultation for migrant workers.

These are proposed programme partnerships, not verified operating services. Structural safety, accessibility, fire egress and long-term stewardship would require development before construction.

05

Coexistence is an everyday practice.

The renderings show care through ordinary acts: preparing food, sharing a table, meeting across generations, watching a performance or learning together. The project imagines multiple cultures being present without asking them to become indistinguishable.

Coexistence is not about integration, but about the dignity of co-presence.
From space to place

The proposal begins with the cultural life that already exists in Zhongli. Its architectural ambition is to offer that life room to continue, grow and be recognised—through a framework that makes care visible within the everyday city.
