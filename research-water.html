Adaptation as an everyday practice.

Abstract

Flood resilience is often described through the exceptional moment of a disaster. This enquiry begins with the ordinary day: the routes to work, spaces of exchange, and shared services that make a neighbourhood habitable. In Kampung Melayu, the space beneath the Ciliwung River viaduct becomes a test of how these relationships might persist as water levels change.

Research question

How can flood-prone infrastructure support civic life through both dry and flood conditions?

Method

The portfolio brings territorial flood research, a multi-scalar design framework, and paired seasonal sections into one study. The sections keep a common architectural frame while changing water levels, circulation, and use. This comparison treats time as a spatial variable: a place is read through more than one environmental state.

Reading exposure together with access

Water is one part of the enquiry. Livelihoods, displacement, public services, and the ability to reach a safe place also shape what resilience means. An elevated platform can address the height of water, but it becomes civic infrastructure only when it connects to daily routines. The research therefore places environmental exposure alongside the relationships that sustain collective life.

A section that changes with the season

Clinics, markets, workshops, and gathering spaces establish a public spine in the dry season. Flood conditions recast parts of the same system as refuge, storage, boat access, and relief space. Wetlands and elevated circulation are considered together rather than as separate landscape and building projects. The design proposition is continuity of access, with different forms of occupation as the river rises.

From a fixed object to an adaptable system

Modular shelters and a repeatable frame allow the proposal to be understood as a system assembled over time. The architectural enquiry shifts toward the relationship between a durable support and changing needs. These drawings describe a design hypothesis; they do not establish measured flood performance or the outcomes of an implemented community programme.

Source & context
