Waterfront+

Shichahai · BeijingUrban design & heritage renewal2024

How can the waterfront reconnect with the neighbourhood behind it?

Design approach

Waterfront+ reconnects the Shichahai water edge with the depth of its lanes, courtyards and shared spaces. The proposal works across Yandai Xiejie, Di’anmen, the Fire God Temple and Baimi Xiejie through selected passages, courtyard adaptation, public programmes and landscape adjustments. It retains differentiated residential thresholds rather than opening every edge uniformly. The project received the 2025 China Human Settlements Academic Year Award, Gold Award in Urban Design.

Gold Award · Urban Design · China Human Settlements Academic Year Award, 2025

01

Reconnect the urban seam.

The overall plan links interventions at the scale of the node, the route and the district. Existing landmarks provide orientation, but the design also addresses the smaller connections that make the waterfront part of ordinary neighbourhood movement. Selected openings and adjusted circulation connect the lake with spaces behind the commercial edge.

Before-and-after drawings distinguish retained elements, adapted structures and new relationships. The strategy does not depend on a continuous new waterfront object. It assembles a sequence in which public landscape, shared facilities and commercial interfaces each have a different role, with domestic areas maintaining their own degree of enclosure.

02

Work within the inherited grain.

The courtyard is the main unit of incremental change. Plans and sections test how shared use can be introduced while retaining the scale and depth of the inherited fabric. The proposed community library, activity centre and shared dining give the connected route destinations beyond visitor consumption.

The waterfront section makes the relationship between water-facing activity and the neighbourhood legible. Changes in level, entry and enclosure differentiate public passage from domestic occupation. This is an urban-design proposal; ownership, resident agreements, conservation review and the operation of new shared facilities remain necessary to develop it into an implementation programme.

Scope & development

Research asks who benefits from greater permeability and how residential continuity can be protected. The project shows the selected spatial interventions through its overall plan, before-and-after studies, courtyards and sections.
